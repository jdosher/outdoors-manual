# Deception Pass

Deception Pass is one of the few spots in Washington where you can catch a hard-fighting coho salmon **from the beach**, no boat needed — and it's the headline shore fishery in Marine Area 8-1. Powerful tidal currents funneling through the narrow pass concentrate baitfish, which in turn concentrate everything that eats them. Coho is the primary draw (peak August–September), but in odd-numbered years pink salmon flood through in massive numbers on their return to the Skagit (next big run: **2027**). Lingcod hold on the rocky structure around the points, sea-run cutthroat work the tide rips, and surfperch are taken from the beach gravel on falling tides. **Be cautious near the water** — currents through the pass run 6–8 knots at peak and are genuinely dangerous; fish from the beaches, not the rocks below the bridges.

## Prime Shore Spots

* **West Beach** — wide gravel beach on the Pacific side of the bridges; salmon casting territory, especially during incoming tide. The classic spot.
* **North Beach** — north side of the pass; calmer than West Beach, productive for coho and pinks.
* **Rosario Beach / Bowman Bay** — quieter, good for sea-run cutthroat and surfperch when the salmon scene is crowded.
* **Note:** The rocks directly below the bridges look fishy and are *very* dangerous — strong currents, slick rocks, fall hazards. Stick to the beaches.

## Tides & Timing (Shore Coho)

**Fish within an hour or two of a tide change — high *or* low.** Local sources agree on the *change*, not on high vs. low. Don't assume "fish the high": that's general Puget Sound beach advice, and it's much stronger for bottom-oriented fish (surfperch, flounder) than for coho, which are pelagic cruisers and run tight to shore at both West and North Beach through the whole cycle.

Ranked by how much they actually matter:

1. **Exchange size — the biggest lever.** A 5 ft exchange fishes well; a 10 ft exchange is a fight you will usually lose from the beach. Check the day's spread before committing to the drive.
2. **Light.** First and last hour of daylight. A tide change landing near sunrise on a small exchange is the combination worth planning a trip around.
3. **Direction.** Lean toward the incoming side of the change.
4. **High vs. low.** Weakest factor of the four — treat both changes as fishable.

**Where you stand changes the math.** West Beach sits outside the pass facing Rosario Strait; it's a normal beach fishery and the 6–8 kt narrows currents mostly don't reach it. North Beach faces into the channel and *is* current-dominated — there, slack matters much more.

## Species & Regulations (2026, MA 8-1)

**MA 8-1 rules differ from MA 9 — verify each species at the [WDFW Marine Area 8-1 page](https://wdfw.wa.gov/fishing/locations/marine-areas/deception-pass-hope-island-skagit-bay) before fishing.**

### Coho (Silver) Salmon — **the primary draw**
* **Peak:** August through September.
* **Retention:** MA 8-1 typically allows coho retention (unlike MA 9). Verify hatchery vs wild rules and daily limits for 2026.
* **Tactics:** Buzz Bombs, Pt. Wilson Darts, casting spoons (Krocodile, Kastmaster 3/4 oz), or float-and-herring on incoming tide. First and last hours of daylight are gold.

### Pink Salmon
* **Run years:** Odd-numbered. **Next strong return: 2027.**
* **Peak:** August.
* **Tactics:** Small pink Buzz Bombs, pink jigs, small pink spoons. Pinks hit aggressively on inbound tide. When they're in, they're stacked.

### Lingcod
* **Season (MA 8-1, 2026):** Verify — typically aligns with MA 9 (May 1 – June 15) but each MA is set separately each year.
* **Size:** 26–36" slot in Puget Sound.
* **Limit:** Check current MA 8-1 rule (typically 1/day).
* **Tactics:** Cast big jigs or swim large soft plastics over rocky structure at slack tide. Lings can be taken from West Beach rocks when tide is manageable.

### Sea-run Cutthroat
* **Best:** Fall through spring.
* **Tactics:** Small streamers, Buzz Bombs, or sand-shrimp on light tackle. Work the tide rips and beach edges.
* **Limit:** Standard statewide saltwater rules (verify; usually no special restrictions in MA 8-1).

### Surfperch
* **Year-round.**
* **Tactics:** Sand shrimp or worms on a high-low rig, cast from beach gravel. Best on falling tide.

### Other species present
Chinook salmon (occasional shore catch), chum salmon (Oct–Nov), Dungeness crab (offshore, boat), smelt (winter beach harvests), herring (baitfish, occasionally landed by snag rigs in season).

### Rockfish
* **Status:** **CLOSED to retention** throughout Puget Sound. Release with descender device.
