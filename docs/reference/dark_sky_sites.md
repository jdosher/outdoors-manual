# Dark Sky Sites for Combo Trips

Camping + fishing + stargazing locations, filtered by travel time from Shoreline. Bortle scale: 1 = darkest, 9 = brightest urban; useful naked-eye Milky Way starts ~3, deep-sky scope work really opens up at 2.

## Combo Sites

### Methow Valley (WA) — Bortle 2–3 — **the primary local target**
Closest serious combo destination from Shoreline (~4–5 hrs via Hwy 20 when open; ~5 hrs via Hwy 2 + Blewett year-round). Eastside rain shadow → far more clear nights than the westside. **The trifecta in one valley:**
- **Astronomy:** Mazama / Winthrop area is genuinely dark; widefield Milky Way unobscured; Andromeda visible naked-eye.
- **Fishing:** Patterson Lake (trout, shore-fishable), Pearrygin Lake State Park (trout, camping on site), Methow River (resident cutthroat).
- **Hunting (fall):** Methow Wildlife Area — California quail, chukar partridge, ruffed and blue grouse. One of the best upland bird destinations in WA.
- **Camping:** Pearrygin Lake SP (developed, lake access), Methow Valley dispersed sites along Twisp and Chewuch river roads.

### Goldendale Observatory area (WA) — Bortle 2
South-central WA, Klickitat County. **IDA-designated International Dark Sky Park.** Camping at Brooks Memorial State Park nearby. Limited fishing emphasis vs Methow, but the observatory itself is worth a visit on its own.

### Sun Lakes / Steamboat Rock (Eastern WA) — Bortle 3
Channeled Scablands south of Coulee Dam. ~4 hrs. Sun Lakes-Dry Falls SP has trout/bass and developed camping. Not the darkest skies on this list but a balanced midpoint.

### Sullivan Lake / Salmo-Priest (NE WA Selkirks) — Bortle 1–2
~6–7 hrs. Closest genuine Bortle-1 site reachable from Seattle. Sullivan Lake holds trout and kokanee; developed lakeside camping. The darkness pays off for galaxy / faint-nebula work — M51 spiral arms, the Veil with an OIII filter, etc.

### Central Idaho Dark Sky Reserve — Stanley / Sawtooth (ID) — Bortle 1
~8–10 hrs. One of the largest dark sky reserves in the world. Sawtooth alpine lakes for cutthroat; Stanley Lake and Redfish Lake camping. Pilgrimage destination — plan around new moon and make it a week.

### Steens Mountain (SE Oregon) — Bortle 1
~10+ hrs. True wilderness sky. Donner und Blitzen River native redband trout. BLM camping at Frenchglen. Long haul, but the kind of darkness most people never see.

## Sites to Skip (Astronomically) Despite Being Close
- **Mountain Loop Hwy** — Everett light dome, Bortle 4–5
- **Hwy 2 corridor (Stevens Pass and lakes)** — same problem
- **Olympic Peninsula west side** — dark on paper, but coastal humidity/fog kills many nights
- **Edmonds Pier / Puget Sound urban shoreline** — Bortle 6–7

## Planning Tools

- **[lightpollutionmap.info](https://www.lightpollutionmap.info/)** — Bortle overlay on any map
- **[darksitefinder.com](https://darksitefinder.com/)** — community-curated dark site database
- **Clear Outside** (app) — cloud cover, transparency, seeing forecast tuned for astronomers
- **Astrospheric** (app) — alternative weather forecasting; some PNW astronomers prefer it
- **Stellarium** (free planetarium) — plan targets the night before

## Gear Notes — 150mm Dobsonian

- Well-matched to car-camping astronomy: no power, minimal setup, packs compactly.
- **Optimal observing window: new moon ± 3 nights.** Plan camping trips around the new-moon calendar — full-moon stargazing is wasted travel.
- Pack: red headlamp, eyepiece case (don't leave eyepieces loose in a car!), observing chair, eyepiece dew shield, planisphere or planetarium app, warm layers (alpine summer nights drop below freezing).
- A simple **Telrad finder** + a 25mm and 10mm eyepiece is enough for one trip; resist over-packing accessories.

## Field Notes
* _Track Bortle estimate (your eyes vs the maps), seeing, transparency, and what was visible. Use this to calibrate which sites are worth which drive times._
