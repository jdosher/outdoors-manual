# Cutthroat Lake

Cutthroat Lake sits at 4,935 ft in a granite cirque directly below Cutthroat Peak, named generations ago for the fish that pack it. The approach is a friendly 2 miles and 500 ft of gain on a well-built but unpaved trail — gentle enough that it's family territory in midsummer. The basin opens up at the lake with broad shoreline on the south side, plenty of clear casting lanes, and a wide-angle view back to Cutthroat Peak's east face. The trail continues past the lake and climbs to Cutthroat Pass, so this can also be the lower leg of a longer day. The fish themselves are small (~7" average Westslope) but abundant and willing — perfect for a kid's first alpine lake or for keeping a few for a pan-fry. Less crowded than Rainy because the trail isn't paved, which filters out the casual tourist traffic.

**Trailhead:** From Washington Pass, drive 4.4 miles east on Hwy 20 and turn left onto the signed paved spur to the Cutthroat Lake trailhead.

## Scouting Report
* **Bank Conditions:** Open shoreline at trail's end, with multiple casting spots around the lake's southern half. Trail continues past the lake toward Cutthroat Pass.
* **Fish:** Abundant Westslope Cutthroat averaging ~7" — small but eager. Perfect pan-fry size; easy fly or small spinner action.
* **Pressure:** Moderate. Less crowded than Rainy because the trail is unpaved.
* **Snow Caveat:** At 4,935 ft, expect snow on the trail and lingering ice on the lake into late June even after Hwy 20 reopens. See [Area Overview](Overview.md) for the corridor-wide pattern; mid-July → September is the safe window.
