# Barclay Lake

Barclay sits in a heavily timbered pocket at the base of Baring Mountain, whose north face climbs nearly 3,000 ft straight up out of the water in a wall of dark rock. The approach is a flat, well-graded forest walk — one of the easiest "real" hikes in the Hwy 2 corridor — winding through mossy second-growth hemlock and Western redcedar. The lake itself is long and shallow on the entry end, deepening as the trail follows the north shore. Old log jams near the outlet and along the timbered bank give you stable platforms to cast from without bushwhacking. Because the trailhead is so accessible, it's popular as a family backpack and gets steady weekend pressure — go midweek or early. Rainbows and brookies share the water; pan-fryers are the rule, not lunkers.

## Scouting Report
* **Bank Conditions:** Highly accessible northern shore via main trail. Abundant stable log jams near the outlet creek provide clean platforms for casting clear of the brush.
* **Pressure:** High on weekends; fish early morning or weekdays for the best shore spots.
