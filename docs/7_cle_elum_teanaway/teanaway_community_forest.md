# Teanaway Community Forest

The Teanaway is a 50,272-acre dual-use working/recreation forest in the central Cascades just north of Cle Elum — **Washington's first state-owned community forest,** acquired by DNR in 2013. It's jointly managed by DNR and WDFW with significant community-board input, which gives it a different feel from purely-USFS lands: more designated infrastructure, more rules, less freelancing. About 47 miles of groomed trails, three designated campgrounds, and a mosaic of meadow / ponderosa pine / dry conifer habitat across the Teanaway River drainage.

The headline difference from typical USFS dispersed-camping country: **no dispersed camping allowed.** Camping is restricted to the three designated campgrounds. That's the trade-off for the protected status and trail investment. Hunting and fishing are open under standard WDFW regulations.

## Scouting Report

### Hunting
* **Mule Deer:** Primary big-game target. Modern firearm general season plus archery and muzzleloader windows — verify current WDFW hunt regulations and unit boundaries.
* **Forest Grouse:** Ruffed and dusky/blue grouse along forest edges and meadow margins. Walking forest roads at dawn is the move.
* **Note:** This is not classic upland chukar/quail country like the Methow — it's a forested-mid-elevation hunt. Different style.

### Fishing
* **Teanaway River and tributaries** hold rainbow and cutthroat. Verify per-stream rules in the WDFW pamphlet — most central-WA streams are selective gear with restrictive trout retention.

### Hiking
* ~47 miles of marked trail. **West Teanaway Trailhead and West Fork Trails opening June 6, 2026** — significant new access.
* Parking at the **Teanaway/29 Pines Staging Area** on North Fork Teanaway Road.

### Astronomy
* Bortle ~3. Better than Stevens Pass corridor, similar to Wenas/Manastash. Find an open meadow or ridge clearing for the Dob — much of the forest is timbered enough to limit sky.
