# Ashland Lakes

The Ashland Lakes are a pair of small, dark waters in the Morningstar Natural Resources Conservation Area — DNR land, not USFS, which is why the pass is Discover and not Northwest Forest. The basin sits in the Puget Sound Convergence Zone and catches an unusual amount of rain and snow, leaving the lakes stained tannic from decaying old-growth duff. The trail is heavily boardwalked to protect the surrounding wetlands; the result is mud-free, structured access over otherwise impossible-to-walk boggy shoreline. Brook trout dominate, with occasional cutthroat stockings, and the fish tend to hold near the bottom in the dark water — weight your bait or run small streamers slow. Upper Ashland is 2.2 miles in with a mild 400 ft of climb; Lower is a few hundred yards earlier. Wooden tent platforms scattered near the lakes make this a quiet overnight option that almost nobody pressures.

## Scouting Report
* **Bank Conditions:** Extensive boardwalks and viewing platforms over boggy shoreline — mud-free casting straight into deeper water.
* **Water:** Dark and tannic from decaying organic material; brookies hold near the bottom.
* **Fish Behavior:** Small but aggressive; great for kids or fly practice.
