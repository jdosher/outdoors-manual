# Quincy Lakes Wildlife Area Unit

The Quincy Lakes Unit is a piece of the larger **Columbia Basin Wildlife Area** (160,100 acres total across the Big Bend of the Columbia) — sage, basalt mesas, scattered seep lakes, and irrigation-edge habitat in classic shrub-steppe country west of George in Grant County. WDFW lists pheasant, chukar, partridge, and quail; the surrounding ag-and-water mosaic is the kind of habitat the District 5 biologist explicitly calls the best chance for quail.

**⚠ Critical access caveat for a trailer trip:** The main access road through Quincy Lakes Wildlife Area is **closed to vehicles October 1 – March 1**. **Foot traffic is allowed year-round.** This means during your entire hunt season, you'll park at the perimeter and walk in. **You will not drive your 26' rig into the unit during the season.** Plan camp accordingly.

## Scouting Report

* **Habitat layers:**
    - Shrub-steppe and sage flats on the rolling terrain
    - Seep lakes and wetland edges in the lower coulees (Quincy Lake, Ancient Lake, Dusty Lake)
    - Irrigation-corridor habitat along the perimeter — the prime quail cover edge
    - Basalt mesas and benches above
* **Quail hot spots (typical):** brushy water/cover edges where irrigation, seep lakes, or natural drainages meet sage
* **Approach with road closure in effect:**
    - Park along Hwy 281 perimeter or off Road 3 NW
    - Walk in 0.5–2 miles to most productive areas
    - Bring a GPS/topo map — open terrain with subtle drainages
* **Companion access:** Frenchman Coulee dispersed camping is adjacent — see [Camping Options](camping_options.md)
